# GlideDeck marketing video: research, message and script

## 1. What the research says

Sources are listed at the end. Reliability is marked, because some popular
presentation "facts" are myths and are deliberately left out.

**Why a canvas instead of slides**

- **Audiences rate zooming presentations higher.** In a randomized Harvard study
  (Moulton, Türkay & Kosslyn, PLOS ONE 2017; 146 presenters, 1,200+ audience members),
  zooming presentations were rated more organized, engaging, persuasive and effective than
  slides, by over half a point on a 5-point scale. Slides were rated no better than
  talking with no visuals at all. *Solid, with caveats: the study used Prezi, was funded by a
  Prezi grant (the authors state Prezi had no role in design or analysis), and found no
  difference in memory or comprehension. So the video claims "more engaging and more
  persuasive", and nothing about learning.*
- **Slides break an argument into fragments on a single path.** Edward Tufte's critique of
  slideware: low resolution, bullet hierarchies, one fixed order, no way to compare. *Solid as
  expert critique.*
- **Overview first, then zoom, then details on demand.** Shneiderman's information-design
  mantra (1996). A zooming canvas is that rule made literal. *Solid as a design principle.*
- **People who author on a canvas build structures closer to the real shape of their
  content** and make better overviews (the "Fly" studies, RWTH Aachen, CHI 2012). *Solid,
  small studies.*
- **People are good at remembering places** (method of loci; Microsoft's Data Mountain).
  *Solid for spatial memory in general; applying it to audiences is an analogy, so the video
  only says every idea "has a home".*

**What Prezi users say** (themes from G2, Capterra and review round-ups; no review is quoted)

| They love | They complain about |
|---|---|
| More engaging and memorable than slides | Dizziness from overdone zooms and spins |
| Stands out, looks polished | Steep learning curve |
| Big picture and detail in one view | Subscription cost, thin free tier |
| Non-linear: follow the conversation | Needs the cloud; offline is paid |
| Storytelling with space | Free-tier decks are public |
| | Lag, and PowerPoint export flattens the zoom |

GlideDeck keeps the left column and answers most of the right: free, open source, offline,
private, native speed, and PowerPoint export that keeps the motion (Morph).

**Left out on purpose:** "30 million PowerPoints a day" and "8-second attention span" are
both unsourced myths.

## 2. The message

**Insight.** Ideas are not a list. They connect, nest and branch. Slides force them into a
line and hide the connections.

**Promise.** Put the whole talk on one infinite canvas and your audience sees how it fits
together: big picture first, then the detail, then back.

**Proof.** A Harvard study, and the product itself: the video is one continuous camera
flight over a canvas, so it demonstrates the idea while it explains it.

**Why GlideDeck.** Everything people love about zooming presentations, without the
subscription, the cloud or the lock-in. Free, open source, 100% offline, and it speaks
PowerPoint.

**Line.** *Stop flipping. Start flying.*

**Tone.** Upbeat, quick, confident, a little playful. Short sentences. No jargon.
